| PMID | Relevance |
|---|---|

| `PMID:24831550` | Trypanocidal assay of diamines and amino alcohols derived from `1-decanol`, `1-dodecanol`, and `1,2-dodecanediol`, not an exact `1,2-dodecanediol` assay. |
| `PMID:33967172` | Directly relevant 2021 `1,2-dodecanediol` combination study against *Staphylococcus aureus* NBRC13276 and *Staphylococcus epidermidis* NBRC12993. |

The 2021 ChEBI-cited mixed-system paper reports MIC and fractional inhibitory
concentration assays for `1,2-dodecanediol` combined with lactic acid, myristic
acid, methylparaben, and isopropyl methylphenol. It names *S. aureus*
NBRC13276 and *S. epidermidis* NBRC12993, defines MIC by lack of turbidity
increase in liquid culture after adding antimicrobial agents, reports a
`3.13-400 ug/mL` `1,2-dodecanediol` concentration range, and reuses the
monomeric `50.0 ug/mL` MIC from a 2019 `1,2-alkanediol` study.

The direct 2019 source is Okukawa et al., `Antibacterial Activity of
1,2-Alkanediol against Staphylococcus aureus and Staphylococcus epidermidis.`,
Journal of Oleo Science 68(8):759-763, DOI `10.5650/jos.ess19074`,
`PMID:31292342`. It evaluates MIC and MBC three times for every condition
against *S. aureus* NBRC13276 and *S. epidermidis* NBRC12993. The MIC assay used
a defined broth and optical density at 660 nm after 24 hours for *S. aureus* or
48 hours for *S. epidermidis* at 37 degrees C. Its result section reports that
the 1,2-hexanediol, 1,2-decanediol, and 1,2-dodecanediol MICs against both
staphylococci were 89600, 300, and `50.0 ug/mL`, respectively. The MBC section
reports 1,2-dodecanediol over `50.0-400 ug/mL` but says both test species grew
under all 1,2-dodecanediol MBC conditions.

The same 2019 paper says a concrete mechanism for 1,2-alkanediol antibacterial
activity was still unclear and frames membrane destruction by surfactant-like
molecules as a prior suggestion, not as an exact molecular target for
1,2-dodecanediol. The 2021 mixed-system paper says the exact mechanism of the
1,2-dodecanediol/myristic acid mixed system was unclear and discusses aggregate
formation near the 1,2-dodecanediol critical micelle concentration as a possible
whole-cell explanation.
